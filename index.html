ONE KEYBOARD PRIVACY POLICY

Effective date: 8 November 2026

One Keyboard ("One Keyboard", "we", "us" or "our") makes One Keyboard, a keyboard app for Android. This Privacy Policy explains what information the app handles, what stays on your phone, what is sent to us or to the companies that provide services to us, how ads work in the app, and the choices you have.

A keyboard sees everything you type. We built One Keyboard so that what you type stays on your phone.

THE SHORT VERSION

  • What you type stays on your phone. We never collect, upload, sell or share your keystrokes, messages, passwords, clipboard, searches or the words the keyboard learns.
  • You don't need an account to use One Keyboard, and we don't sell your personal information.
  • We collect usage statistics and crash reports (through Google Firebase) to fix bugs and improve the app. They never include what you type, copy or search, or which apps you type in.
  • One Keyboard is free and may show ads. Ads come from advertising partners, who may use your device's advertising ID.
  • We measure how many people finish setting up the keyboard, and whether our own advertising works, with app events.
  • Some features you choose connect to other services: web search (Google), sticker and language downloads, turning your location into an address (Android), and your phone's autofill service.

1. WHO WE ARE

One Keyboard is published on Google Play by One Keyboard, which is responsible for the information described in this policy. For any question or request about privacy, write to hellopratiksagar@gmail.com.

2. WHAT YOU TYPE

The keyboard works on your phone. Suggestions, corrections, gesture typing, emoji suggestions, typing Indian languages in English letters, and stylish fonts all run on your phone. What you type is not sent to us or to anyone else.

What the keyboard remembers, it keeps on your phone. To suggest your words, the keyboard learns words you type and keeps the words you add to your personal dictionary. These stay in the app's private storage on your phone and are never uploaded.

Password and private fields. In password fields, the keyboard doesn't learn from what you type, doesn't suggest words, doesn't offer your clipboard and doesn't offer web search. In fields that an app marks as private (incognito), the keyboard doesn't learn from or keep anything you type.

Smart suggestions read only a little, and only on your phone. Some features react to where you're typing: the paste suggestion, your address or number when you type "my address" or "my number", places above address fields, quick replies in ride and delivery apps, and typing animations and your keyboard buddy reacting to what you type (for example "happy birthday" or "haha"). For these, the keyboard looks at which app you're typing in, what kind of field it is (for example email, phone number or chat), whether the field is empty, and at most the last 32 characters before the cursor. This happens only on your phone, never in password or private fields, and is never kept, logged or sent.

About Android's warning. When you turn on a keyboard that didn't come with your phone, Android warns that the keyboard may be able to collect the text you type, including passwords and credit card numbers. Android shows this message for every such keyboard. It doesn't mean that One Keyboard collects what you type; as this policy explains, it doesn't.

3. INFORMATION THAT STAYS ON YOUR PHONE

The app keeps the following only on your phone, in its private storage. We never receive it.

  • Words the keyboard learns as you type, and the next words it predicts, for better suggestions. To delete them, clear the app's storage (Android Settings > Apps > One Keyboard > Storage > Clear storage) or uninstall the app.
  • Your personal dictionary (words and shortcuts you add), to suggest your own words. Delete words in Settings > Personal dictionary.
  • Clipboard history and quick replies, for the clipboard panel and the paste suggestion. Clips you don't pin are deleted after the time you choose (1 hour, 1 day or 1 week). You can delete clips or use "Clear recent clips" in the clipboard panel, or turn clipboard history off.
  • My details: your name, phone number and email addresses, if you enter them, to offer them above matching fields. Remove them in Settings > My details.
  • Saved places (such as home and work), if you add them, to offer them above address fields. Remove them in Settings > Saved places.
  • Stickers you make, and the photo you pick for a photo theme. Delete stickers in the Stickers tab, and remove the photo in Themes > Remove photo theme.
  • Names from your contacts, if you allow contacts access, to suggest names as you type. Turn off "Suggest contact names" in Settings, or remove the contacts permission.
  • Your contacts' phone numbers, only if you turn on "Suggest contacts' numbers" (off by default), to suggest a contact's number when you type their name. They're kept only in memory while needed, dropped when you turn the option off, and never saved.
  • The list of apps on your phone, and which apps you open from app suggestions, to suggest apps when you type in your home screen's search.
  • Your keyboard buddy, if you turn it on: which buddy you chose, on how many days it was out with you (to unlock new buddies), and whether you've seen its invitation. Turn it off in Settings > Keyboard buddy.
  • Your settings, chosen languages and theme, recent emoji and stickers, and which tips you've seen, to remember your choices.

Your clipboard, details, saved places, stickers and photo theme are kept in storage that Android unlocks only after you first unlock your phone, so they can't be read on the lock screen.

Backups. If Android backup is on for your phone, Android may copy the app's settings (such as your theme, languages and keyboard options) to your Google account and to a new phone. Your learned words, clipboard, details, places, stickers and photos are not included. You control Android backup in your phone's settings.

4. INFORMATION WE COLLECT

We don't ask for your name, email address or phone number, and the app has no accounts. We collect the following, linked only to random identifiers created by the app's services.

Usage statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

  • What you do in the app, not what you type: which features and screens you use and how often (for example, opening the emoji panel, sending a sticker or changing a setting), and the steps of setting up the keyboard.
  • Your choices: your keyboard theme, your keyboard languages and how many you use.
  • Collected automatically by Firebase: app version, device model, Android version, the phone's language, approximate country or region (worked out from your IP address), when you first opened the app, how long you use it, an app instance ID and, where available, your advertising ID.

Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics)

When the app crashes or has a serious error, we receive a report with the error and the place in our code where it happened, the app version, device model, Android version, free memory and storage, the time, and a Crashlytics installation ID. When our dictionary engine crashes, we receive only a short summary (the kind of crash and the names of our functions involved), never a copy of the app's memory, because that memory can hold the word being typed.

Setup measurement (Meta App Events)

To see how many people finish setting up the keyboard, and whether our advertising leads to installs, we send Meta these events: which setup steps you reach (opening the app, turning on and choosing the keyboard, choosing languages and a theme, finishing setup), the languages and theme you choose during setup, and the first time the keyboard opens. Meta's software also receives device information, such as the device model and Android version, and your advertising ID. We don't send Meta screen titles, links, or anything you type.

Ads

When the app shows ads, our advertising partners collect the information described in section 5.

What we never collect

  • What you type: words, messages, passwords, payment details.
  • Your clipboard, and your emoji, sticker and web searches.
  • Your contacts, your location, your photos, your details or your saved places.
  • Which apps you type in.

Usage statistics and crash reports are part of the app. We need them to keep One Keyboard working and to improve it, so the app has no setting to turn them off. You can object to them at any time (see section 12).

5. ADS

One Keyboard is free, and we may show ads to keep it free. Ads may appear in the app's screens (such as the home screen, themes and stickers) and in the keyboard (such as in the toolbar or among suggestions, for example a sponsored app suggestion). Ads are marked as ads or as sponsored.

Ads are provided by advertising partners. When an ad is shown, the advertising partner may collect your advertising ID, device information (such as the device model, Android version, language and screen size), your IP address and the approximate location worked out from it, the app's name and version, and how you interact with the ad (for example, whether you see or tap it). Advertising partners use this to show ads, to limit how often you see the same ad, to measure how ads perform, to prevent fraud and, where the law allows and with your consent where it's required, to show ads based on your interests.

Your ad choices:

  • Delete or reset your advertising ID (see section 11). Without it, advertising partners can't show you ads based on your interests; you'll still see ads, but they may be less relevant.
  • In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, we ask for your consent where the law requires it before ads based on your interests are shown, and you can change your choice at any time in the app.

We will name our advertising partners, with links to their privacy policies, in section 10 of this policy before their ads appear in the app.

6. HOW WE USE INFORMATION

We use the information described above to:

  • provide the keyboard and its features;
  • find and fix crashes and bugs;
  • understand which features are used, so we can improve them and decide what to build next;
  • show ads, measure them and keep the app free;
  • measure how many people complete setup, and whether our own advertising works;
  • change the app's settings and turn features on gradually (see "Remote settings" in section 7);
  • keep the app and its users safe, prevent fraud, and meet legal obligations.

We don't sell your personal information, and we don't build profiles of what you type.

7. FEATURES THAT CONNECT TO OTHER SERVICES

Remote settings (Firebase Remote Config). From time to time the app downloads its settings from Google Firebase, for example where sticker packs are stored or which features are on. The request includes the app version, basic device information and a Firebase installation ID.

Push messages (Firebase Cloud Messaging). The app receives silent messages that we send to every user at once, for example to refresh its settings. We don't send messages to individual users, and the app doesn't send its message token to us. The app shows a notification only if you allow notifications.

Sticker packs and word lists. When you open the stickers panel, the app downloads sticker packs from One Keyboard's sticker server (currently hosted on GitHub Pages). When the keyboard has a language that people also type in English letters (such as Kannada or Tamil) together with English (India), the app downloads that language's word list from the same kind of server, so English (India) knows its words. As with any website, the host sees your IP address and which files are downloaded. The app sends nothing about you.

Web search. Where it's available, the search button in the keyboard's toolbar opens Google's image search inside the keyboard, after a notice that your searches go to Google. What you search for and the pages you see go directly to Google, under Google's Privacy Policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy). One Keyboard doesn't read the page, and doesn't log, keep or send your searches. The search panel's cookies, history and cache are deleted when you close it. If you send a picture, the app downloads it from the website it's on. Web search isn't shown in password fields, and you can turn it off in Settings.

Your location, only when you ask. When you tap Location in the keyboard's menu, "Current location" above an address field, or a location reply in a ride app's chat, the app gets your location from Android's location service and turns it into an address with Android's address lookup, which on most phones uses Google's servers. The app types the address (and, where available, a map link) into your field, and doesn't keep, log or send your location itself. It never uses your location in the background.

Autofill suggestions. On Android 11 and later, your phone's autofill service (such as Google or your password manager) can show its suggestions, such as saved passwords or codes, in the keyboard's toolbar. The keyboard only displays them and can't read them; when you tap one, your autofill service fills it in. You can turn this off in Settings.

Photos. For stickers and photo themes, Android's photo picker gives the app only the photo you pick. The app keeps its own copy on your phone (for stickers, only the finished sticker) and uploads nothing.

8. PERMISSIONS

  • Internet and network status: usage statistics, crash reports, remote settings, push messages, sticker and language downloads, web search and ads.
  • Contacts (optional): suggest names from your contacts, and their numbers if you turn that on. Asked only if you choose to; you can say no.
  • Location (optional, only while in use): type your current location or address when you ask. Asked the first time you use it; never used in the background.
  • Notifications (optional): show a notification when a feature needs one. Asked the first time a feature shows one.
  • Vibration: feedback when you press keys.
  • Run at startup: get the keyboard ready after your phone restarts.
  • Personal dictionary: read and add words to Android's own personal dictionary.
  • Advertising ID, ad attribution and install referrer: showing and measuring ads, and measuring installs, through Google's, Meta's and our advertising partners' software (see sections 5 and 11).
  • Receive push messages and keep the phone awake briefly: receive push messages through Google Play services.
  • See apps with a home screen icon: suggest apps when you type in your home screen's search. This isn't a permission Android asks about.

You can change optional permissions at any time in Android Settings > Apps > One Keyboard > Permissions.

9. HOW LONG WE KEEP INFORMATION

  • Usage statistics: kept by Google Analytics for up to 14 months, then deleted. Reports that don't identify anyone may be kept longer.
  • Crash reports: kept by Firebase Crashlytics for 90 days.
  • Setup measurement: kept by Meta under Meta's own terms.
  • Ads: kept by our advertising partners under their own terms.
  • On your phone: until you delete it, clear the app's storage or uninstall the app. Unpinned clipboard clips are deleted after the time you choose.

Uninstalling One Keyboard deletes everything it keeps on your phone. Copies of its settings in your Android backup follow your Google account's backup settings.

We don't sell your personal information. We share information only:

We may also publish statistics that don't identify anyone, such as the number of people using a language.

11. YOUR ADVERTISING ID

Your Android phone has an advertising ID that apps can use for ads. Google Analytics for Firebase, Meta's software and our advertising partners may read it, to show and measure ads and to see whether our own advertising leads to installs.

You can reset or delete your advertising ID at any time: on most phones, Android Settings > Privacy > Ads, or Settings > Google > Ads. Once it's deleted, these services can't use it, and ads won't be based on your interests.

15. SECURITY

Everything the app sends travels encrypted over HTTPS. What the app keeps is in its private storage, which other apps can't read. Your personal items are kept in storage that Android encrypts and unlocks only after you first unlock your phone, and the keyboard never shows them on the lock screen. No method of storing or sending information is completely secure, but we work to protect yours.

We don't knowingly collect personal information from children or show them ads based on their interests. If you believe a child has given us personal information, write to us and we'll delete it.
